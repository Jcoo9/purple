What we are

Purple is America's voice. Not the media's version of it. Not a politician's version of it. The actual thing — what real people think, in their own words, on the issues that shape their lives.

We built Purple because the loudest voices in America aren't the most representative ones. Cable news tells you what to think. Social media rewards outrage. Polls give you numbers without faces. Nobody was building the thing in the middle — a place where a teacher from Ohio, a veteran from Texas, and a nurse from New Jersey all have exactly the same standing.

That place is Purple.

"Most Americans are not as divided as the loudest voices make us seem."
— Purple, 2025
How it works

Vote. A spectrum of four positions. No spin. Anonymous. Takes seconds.

Speak. Record your take in under 60 seconds. Unedited. Unfiltered. Your name builds a leaderboard score.

Filter. Slice results by state, age, background, and community. See how Veterans voted versus Educators.

Track. The same question asked across rounds. The delta between January and March is the story nobody else is telling.

The name

Red and blue mixed together make purple. It is not a compromise. It is what you get when you stop pretending only one color is real.

The origin

Purple was conceived by two friends who shared a conviction that American public discourse had lost its connection to ordinary people. One of them passed away before seeing it built. Purple carries his belief that real voices matter, that the truth lives somewhere in the middle, and that the right technology can surface it.
